# AI Video Editor: enhancement scan

_What to add, what to ignore. Scan of Higgsfield, the AI video-editing tool landscape,
technique research per known gap, and the ad-performance layer. 2026-08-18._

# What to add to the AI Video Editor, and what to ignore

## 1. The short answer

Almost nothing in the vendor landscape is worth buying, because the three highest-value additions are things we already have the data to compute for free: hook variant fan-out, a deterministic QC gate, and keyframed motion and audio automation written into the XML instead of baked into pixels. The one genuine capability purchase is a cheap semantic index over the source footage (Gemini native video input, roughly 2 cents an ad, cached), which closes three of our named gaps at once and turns the tool from a storyboard executor into something that knows what was actually filmed. Everything sold as a virality score, a style preset, a restyle, an avatar, or a one-click reframe is either noise, a competitor to our own output, or someone else's version of Ricardo's 10 percent, and the correct response to all of it is no.

The scan surfaced one hard fact worth internalising: Resolve 21 does script-to-timeline take matching for $295 one-time, and Threadline does intonation-aware take selection with native XML export for $24/mo. The assembly is no longer our moat. The storyboard-as-spec intake, the DTC ad rules, and the honesty of the handoff are.

## 3. The one big unlock: a persistent semantic index of the footage

Everything else on that table makes the existing product better. This one changes what it can do, because right now the tool is blind to its own inputs. The storyboard is treated as truth and the footage is assumed to comply, which is why a named-but-unfilmed shot silently becomes a talking head, why b-roll matching leans on filenames in `broll_catalog.json`, and why `pick_broll_window.py` can find motion but cannot tell whether it found the hand reaching for the jar or the hand putting it down. Fix the sampling before you touch the model: 8 stills across a 12 second clip is one frame every 1.5 seconds and no amount of model quality recovers a beat from that.

The build. Run `PySceneDetect` first so no analysis window straddles a shot boundary. Then one pass per clip using Gemini's native video input, `fps=1` at low media resolution, which is about 100 tokens per second of video and puts a 12 second clip near a tenth of a cent, returning a structured record per clip: subjects present, objects and product, on-screen text, shot type, framing, lighting, dominant motion, and a list of action phases with `MM:SS` boundaries. Persist that keyed on the file content hash, in a `footage_index.json` next to the existing `broll_catalog.json` or a small SQLite file so it survives across jobs and shoots, which means a re-run costs zero and a client's second ad from the same shoot costs zero. Then rewire three consumers to query the index instead of guessing: `match_broll.py` matches storyboard scene text against tags rather than filenames and reports a confidence, the missing-shot detector becomes a straight set difference (storyboard asks for "close-up of pump dispensing", no clip carries those tags, emit a `MISSING` record), and `pick_broll_window.py` uses the index to pick a 2 second candidate window and then refines to the exact frame deterministically with optical flow, backing off to the first frame after the flow peak where magnitude drops below 60 percent of it for "action completed and settled". Keep the existing OpenCV heuristic as the keyless offline path so the pipeline never hard-depends on a network call, and cache aggressively so the model is not in the runtime loop in any meaningful sense. Roughly a week of work, about two cents an ad, and it is metadata only, so it cannot violate the boundary. Downstream it is also the substrate for the QC gate (diff the index of the burned preview against the storyboard) and for a cross-client b-roll library that lets us reuse footage a strategist forgot existed.

Hook variant fan-out is the bigger revenue item and it is item 2 on the table for that reason. But it is a for-loop over capability we already have. This is new capability.

## 4. Generated footage: yes, narrowly, and never automatically

**Straight answer: yes for silent product and object inserts, no for anything involving a person, and never as an automatic step inside the pipeline.**

Seedance and Veo at $0.24 to $1.05 a shot are genuinely good enough for a locked-off product insert. Three specifics make that real rather than hopeful: `camera_fixed` on Seedance v1 pro-fast gives a locked-off shot, which is exactly what intercuts cleanly with handheld iPhone footage because it removes the AI-motion tell; `/veo3.1/first-last-frame-to-video` lets you pin both ends so the insert starts and ends on frames adjacent to the real shots; and feeding the client's actual product photo as `start_image` or `image_references` is what keeps the label truthful, which is already a hard production rule for us.

**Acceptable:** the product sitting on a counter, a pour, powder going into a glass, a texture macro, packaging beauty. Silent, always, with `generate_audio: false`. Under about two seconds on screen. Generated from the client's real product photo, never from a text description of the product. Landed in the XML on a track named `GENERATED`, marked in the handoff notes, and approved by a human (strategist or Ricardo) before it ships. Budget 2 to 3 attempts, so call it $1 to $3 per usable insert.

**Where it embarrasses us:** any human skin, any hands interacting with the product, and any shot the ad's claim rests on. Ricardo's own MaxFusion writeup is the evidence, not my caution: "a normal person holding a product in a normal apartment" read "slightly rendered and off", and Seedance 2.5 audio mangles syllables in a way you cannot prompt away. Add to that any product whose label needs to be legible, any regulated category (supplements, health, finance), any client with an AI-disclosure policy, EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations live since 2 August 2026, and the fact that the market is moving the other way. Icon rebranded from an AI avatar ad tool to "The Human Admaker" and now sells human-filmed UGC at $1,000 for six ads, marketing the footage as explicitly not AI. A client who bought "real creator footage, assembled" and finds a generated frame in the cut has a legitimate grievance, and it does not matter that the frame was cheap.

**The consequence for the build order:** the fix for the missing-shot gap is item 1 on my table, not item 10. Declaring the hole is worth more than filling it, costs nothing, and takes a day. Generation is an optional, manual, human-approved fill on top of that. Build them in that order and never let the second one fire without a human saying yes.

## 5. What to ignore

**Baked pixels, non-negotiable refusals.** Runway Gen-4 Aleph relight and object removal at $0.15/sec, because relighting is the grade and every output is an opaque MP4 that can only be regenerated. Premiere's Generative Extend and Firefly Text to Video, baked frames on metered credits. Higgsfield Shorts Studio, which restyles every frame. Higgsfield Personal Clipper and all of Lane 1's burned captions, which destroy the exact thing our handoff is worth money for. Captions.ai "AI Edit" presets, which are Ricardo's grade and caption styling sold as a button. Eye-contact correction, which silently rewrites the performer's face.

**Products that replace the designer or replace us.** Higgsfield Marketing Studio, minimum 12 seconds, wants avatars and hooks, outputs a finished ad for $7.20; it competes with our product rather than feeding it. HeyGen and Argil avatars, which replace the creator's footage entirely and sit upstream of our whole product. Dubbing, `voice_change` and lip-sync anywhere in the pipeline, because they re-render video and re-merge audio onto pixels; a Spanish variant is a new XML against the same sources with a new VO track, and if a client wants that it is a separate product line.

**Scores.** Higgsfield's Virality Predictor is free and I would still not wire it. Its "brain regions" are axis-aligned bounding boxes on a template FreeSurfer mesh, it samples one frame per second, it is capped at 15 second inputs so it cannot ingest most of our deliverables, its own payload disagrees with itself (`scores.overall_score` 43 versus `summary.overall_score` 33 on the same job), and it gave a competent real ad with a strong physical hook a hook score of 26 out of 100. Opus Clip's 0 to 99 publishes no weighting, no training data and no accuracy figure. Pencil's numbers come only through secondary sources and predict CTR, not retention. Calibrate all of it against the published academic ceiling: on SnapUGC, 120,651 labelled videos, the best ensemble reaches Spearman 0.71 against real retention, leaving half the variance unexplained, and every vendor is doing less than that with less data. Against Motion's measured base rate that only about 5 percent of ads ever spend 10x their account median, an undefined "83 percent accuracy" is close to uninformative. A score is a triage order, never a gate. And absolutely do not adopt Higgsfield's advertised generate, score, recreate loop, which is pixel generation driven by a number nobody can defend.

**Cost without proportional gain.** Higgsfield Reframe at $8.46 for 15 seconds and $33.48 for 60, to outpaint footage we already control and normalize for free with ffmpeg. Upscale, where pricing could not even be verified. TwelveLabs at $0.042/min indexing is fine engineering but only worth it if we commit to a persistent searchable library, and Gemini native video does the per-job answer for a fraction. Enterprise creative scoring (Vidmob, CreativeX, Neurons) publishes no price, no auditable validation, and runs 6 to 12 week implementations. Meta's Ad Library API is free and a dead end for us, since general commercial DTC ads only return if they ran in the EU.

**Needs a model or a GPU in our runtime.** Higgsfield's Video Analysis is the best thing on their platform for us and it is free, but it is MCP-only, which means running an MCP client on the VPS and depending on an unpriced preview tier for a load-bearing QA step. Use it manually for competitor-ad ingestion via `youtube_url`, do not put it in the pipeline. Open-weight VLMs that emit timestamps (Qwen3-VL, VideoChat3) need a GPU the CPU-only runtime does not have. `line/lighthouse` moment retrieval is trained on minutes-long consumer video for 5 to 30 second moments and will roughly reproduce what our OpenCV heuristic already does. And do not average CLIP or SigLIP-2 frame embeddings and call it video retrieval; benchmarked at 0.325 NDCG@10 it is fine for "which clip" and useless for "which instant".

**Do not rebuild.** Multicam switching is a commoditized $29/mo Premiere plugin, it needs per-speaker audio we do not control, and it is not our 15 to 60 second vertical format. Buy AutoPod when a client pays for interview work.

**One client-facing item.** Meta Advantage+ Creative has launched every new Sales, Leads and App Promotion campaign with all enhancements on by default since February 2026, including auto brightness and saturation, adding and repositioning text overlays, adding music from Meta's library, and auto-cropping. Meta will bake the designer's 10 percent at serve time. It does not corrupt our XML, it corrupts the client's read on whether our cut worked. Put a line in the handoff telling the buyer which enhancements to disable so the retention numbers describe the ad we actually shipped.

## 6. Staged plan

**This week (all small, all zero cost)**
1. Do the Premiere round-trip test that pins `<when>` semantics: keyframe two points on a clip in Premiere, export FCP7 XML, read the values back against that clip's `in`/`out`/`start`, hardcode the answer. Thirty minutes, and it unblocks both the ducking envelope and the push-in.
2. Kill the silent talking-head fallback in `match_broll.py`. Unmatched storyboard scene becomes a `MISSING` record in `status.json` and a line in the handoff notes.
3. Finish the in-flight audio work against stated numbers rather than by ear: two-pass `loudnorm`, VO stem -16 LUFS, preview mix -14 LUFS with a -1.0 dBTP ceiling, verified with `ebur128=peak=true`. The ceiling matters more than the LUFS figure because low-bitrate AAC clips above it, and neither Meta nor TikTok publishes an official target so precision beyond consistent-and-not-clipped is wasted.
4. Three QC asserts, added to `package_handoff.py`: frame 0 is not black, every clipitem `pathurl` resolves, no `silencedetect` hit over 400ms where the word timeline says words exist.
5. Install the Higgsfield Adobe plugin on Ricardo's machine and let him tell us whether Reframe and Upscale are worth anything in practice.

**This month**
6. Hook variant fan-out. Sibling XMLs with identical body frame numbers, named `<brand>_<concept>_H1..H3`, drawing the 2nd and 3rd best hook delivery from `pick_takes.py` plus one caption-only variant. Test the single-level nested `Body` sequence, fall back to siblings if the importer disappoints. This is the item the client will pay for.
7. Full `qc.json` with every check, its measured value and its threshold, and a hard refusal to package on fail. Include the self-consistency check: re-transcribe the burned preview and diff against the storyboard with `jiwer`, fail a scene over roughly 15 percent WER. That single check catches the class of failure that actually reaches clients.
8. `audiolevels` ducking keyframes from word timings, in both `build_assembly_xml.py` and `build_premiere_xml.py`. About 60 lines each. Same envelope burned into the preview.
9. Punch-cut emphasis split (zero keyframes, most editable representation), then the Basic Motion slow push now that `<when>` is known.
10. Voice isolation as an additional linked audio track, ElevenLabs at $0.12/min.
11. Start emitting the measured-properties row per delivered ad. It is worthless today and it is the only proprietary asset on this page in six months.
12. Pick a music path: pull the free Soundstripe 30-day API key from Account > API and try Supe against a real brief. Note before evaluating Epidemic that their partner terms forbid caching track metadata locally, which conflicts with a locally tagged shortlist.

**This quarter**
13. Build the footage index (section 3) and rewire `match_broll.py`, the missing-shot detector, and `pick_broll_window.py` to consume it. Cache by content hash, keep the OpenCV fallback.
14. Ship the strategist review surface so a wrong-take revision costs a click instead of Ricardo's afternoon.
15. Get read access to one client's Meta ad insights and join eight to ten weeks of our measured properties against their per-ad thumbstop and 15s/3s retention. A predictor calibrated on our own cuts for our own clients, with a winner rate we know is around 5 percent, is worth more than anything on the market and nobody can sell it to us.
16. Evaluate OpenTimelineIO as the internal edit representation with FCP7 as an output adapter. It is JSON, so an agent can read and revise a timeline, and it buys Premiere, Resolve and Final Cut for free. Not urgent, because Premiere's own MCP server is immature and the XML file handoff is still the right delivery mechanism.
17. Generated product inserts, manual and human-approved only, over Higgsfield REST, on a `GENERATED` track. Only after item 13 makes the hole visible in the first place.

**Explicitly not this quarter:** multicam templatization, tracked reframe, any predictor or score, and any automatic generation step.
