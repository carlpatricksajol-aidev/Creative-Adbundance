# DR Scorecard: The 10-Parameter Script Review

This is the rubric the Creative Strategist scores every script against at Step 4, before anything
reaches a client. It exists because a real client graded a real AI-written batch and the four scores
that came back lowest were the four that decide whether a direct-response script works at all:
Proof Matching Narrative Promise 5.5, Offer Placement 5.5, Product Introduction 6.5,
Differentiation Across Concepts 6.5. Those four now carry a higher bar than the rest.

Score as a strategist who did not write the draft. If you wrote it, you are not allowed to be kind
to it. The only currency in this review is quoted lines from the script.

## The 10 parameters at a glance

| # | Parameter | Pass | Scope | Failed-batch baseline |
|---|-----------|------|-------|-----------------------|
| 1 | Proof Matching Narrative Promise | >= 8 | per script | 5.5 |
| 2 | Offer Placement | >= 8 | per script | 5.5 |
| 3 | Product Introduction | >= 8 | per script, cross-checked across batch | 6.5 |
| 4 | Differentiation Across Concepts | >= 8 | batch, recorded on every script | 6.5 |
| 5 | Hook Distinctness | >= 7 | per script (3 hooks against each other) | not separately graded |
| 6 | Mechanism Clarity | >= 7 | per script | not separately graded |
| 7 | Number Specificity and Believability | >= 7 | per script | not separately graded |
| 8 | Compliance Safety | >= 7 | per script | not separately graded |
| 9 | Visual Proof Integration | >= 7 | per script | not separately graded |
| 10 | CTA Closing Strength | >= 7 | per script | not separately graded |

Score in whole numbers per script. Halves appear only in batch averages, which is how the client
reported the failed batch. A batch ships only when every script clears every threshold on its own
row. One script at 7 on Offer Placement fails the batch; you do not average your way past a gate.

---

## 1. Proof Matching Narrative Promise (pass >= 8)

**What it measures:** whether the proof beat closes the exact argument the hook opened, in the same
unit of value the concept contract promised.

Read the contract line first: "This ad tests whether [PRODUCT] can [SPECIFIC PROMISE] for [PERSONA],
proven by [MEASURABLE OUTCOME]." Then read only the proof beat. If you cannot reconstruct the
contract from the proof, this parameter is failing no matter how good the numbers sound.

**Automatic ceiling:** if the contract promised bookings, consults, orders, signed clients or
inquiries and the proof stops at comments, DMs, views, followers or "growth", the score is capped
at 3. That is the single most common failure in AI-written scripts and it is not a rounding error,
it is a different ad.

- **1-3:** Proof is platform activity standing in for business outcome. The contract promised
  launch-day orders and the script ends on "my comments blew up". Or the proof is qualitative
  ("people were actually interested") with no measurable outcome at all. Or there is a number but
  it measures the wrong thing entirely.
- **4-6:** Proof reaches the right category but stops one rung short or arrives out of order. Reach
  and engagement are there, the business outcome is implied rather than stated ("and things picked
  up from there"). Or the outcome is stated but the ladder below it is missing, so the number has
  nothing holding it up.
- **7-8:** Full ladder present and in order: reach metric, then engagement metric, then business
  outcome, and the business outcome is the exact unit named in the contract. The hook's question is
  answered by the proof's number.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the proof carries the comparison the hook set up. The failed first launch,
  the empty off-season, the cold outbound that produced nothing, all get their counter-number, so
  the proof reads as a resolved argument rather than a stat drop. A reviewer could quote the hook
  line and the proof line side by side and see one answer the other.

**Evidence required:** quote the contract line, the hook line, and the proof line. All three go in
your notes even when the score passes.

---

## 2. Offer Placement (pass >= 8)

**What it measures:** whether the offer enters early, inside the product-introduction beat, in the
speaker's own voice, and is repeated as a CTA Overlay end card.

**Automatic ceiling:** offer appears for the first time in the final sentence, score capped at 2.
Offer appears only in the end-card overlay and never in spoken copy, capped at 4.

- **1-3:** The offer is the last thing said and nothing before it hints that money or terms exist.
  The body of the script would be identical if the product were free or $10,000.
- **4-6:** The offer appears before the end but as a bolted-on sentence with no narrative work
  ("Right now it's 50% off."). Delete it and the story is unchanged. Or it appears mid-script but as
  an announcement in brand voice rather than as something the speaker did.
- **7-8:** The offer is woven into the moment of adoption, in first person and past tense, as an
  action the speaker took: "I grabbed their annual plan while it was 50% off." A CTA Overlay end
  card repeats the same terms, and the phrasing of that repeat is not word-for-word identical to any
  other script in the batch.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the offer terms do narrative work. The reason for taking the offer is tied
  to the persona's actual constraint (one launch window, a dead off-season, a trial budget), so the
  offer is part of why the story resolved. The end-card repeat reads as a callback to that moment,
  not a pasted line.

**Evidence required:** quote the offer sentence and give its position as a fraction of the script
(for example "sentence 6 of 14"). Anything past the two-thirds mark should not be scoring 8.

---

## 3. Product Introduction (pass >= 8)

**What it measures:** whether the product enters through a construction the narrative earned, and
whether that construction is unique inside the batch.

**Automatic ceiling:** the construction "That's where [PRODUCT] came in" (or any near-identical
pivot phrase) appears, capped at 4. The same construction appears in a second script in the batch,
both scripts capped at 5.

- **1-3:** Generic pivot with no cause ("That's when I found [PRODUCT]"), placed wherever the setup
  ran out. The product arrives because the script needed it to, not because anything in the story
  produced it. Or the product arrives past the halfway point after long atmospheric setup.
- **4-6:** There is a reason for the discovery but it is the default one and it recurs across the
  batch (a vague "someone told me about it"). The introduction is functional but interchangeable
  between scripts.
- **7-8:** The introduction is determined by this specific narrative and could not be lifted into
  another script in the batch. Concrete routes: a named peer recommendation with a reason attached,
  "I took it off my to-do list and handed it over", "I flipped the order and did discovery first",
  a second discovery channel found after the first one failed. The product lands in the first half.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the introduction carries the persona's voice and situation so tightly that
  it doubles as characterisation, and it sets up the mechanism line that follows without repeating
  it. The offer rides inside this beat (see parameter 2) without the sentence feeling crowded.

**Evidence required:** quote the introduction sentence from every script in the batch, side by side,
in your notes. That side-by-side is also the input to parameter 4.

---

## 4. Differentiation Across Concepts (pass >= 8)

**What it measures:** whether each script is testing its own concept, or whether the batch has
collapsed into one template wearing different hooks.

**How to score it:** run the batch-level swap test. For every pair of scripts, ask "could these two
swap middle sections without anyone noticing?" The middle section means the mechanism line, the
trust line and the proof beat. Then check that no two scripts share a product-introduction
construction or an offer phrasing. Score once per batch and record the same score on every script's
row, then subtract 1 from the specific script that is the offender if one script is dragging the
set.

**Automatic ceiling:** any pair of scripts passes the swap test cleanly (the swap is undetectable),
capped at 4 for the whole batch.

- **1-3:** All scripts run the same spine: problem, the tool finds relevant people, real engagement,
  no bots, offer at the end. Middles are fully interchangeable. The only variation is the opening
  situation and the persona's job title.
- **4-6:** Hooks and personas differ, and one or two beats differ, but the mechanism line and the
  trust line are the same thought in every script and the proof beats use the same metric family.
  A swap would be noticed only by someone reading closely.
- **7-8:** Every script has its own mechanism framing, its own trust-line placement or wording, and a
  proof beat in its own unit of value. No pair survives a swap: moving a middle would break the
  script's own contract. No shared product-introduction constructions, no shared offer phrasings.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the concepts are testing genuinely different arguments, so the batch
  produces readable learning. If script A wins and script B loses, the client learns something about
  the market, not something about the copywriter. Each script's dominant message maps to a different
  row of the concept contract sheet.

**Evidence required:** list the pairs you tested and one sentence per pair on what makes the swap
detectable.

---

## 5. Hook Distinctness (pass >= 7)

**What it measures:** whether the three hooks are three different entry points (different pain, goal,
persona or root cause) rather than one thought reworded three times.

**Automatic ceiling:** hooks are not labelled with their angle, capped at 6 regardless of quality.
Two of three hooks resolve to the same angle label, capped at 5.

- **1-3:** Three paraphrases of one sentence. "Nobody showed up to my launch" / "My launch was dead"
  / "I launched to silence". Same pain, same persona, same root cause. Labels missing or
  invented after the fact to make the set look varied.
- **4-6:** Two genuinely different entry points and one duplicate, or three different phrasings that
  all attack the same root cause from the same emotional register. Labels present but generic
  ("Hook 2: Problem").
- **7-8:** Three distinct entry points, each labelled in house form ("Hook 1: Empty Launch / Pain
  Point", "Hook 2: Off-Season Gap / Goal", "Hook 3: Wrong Audience / Root Cause"), each with its own
  Overlay and Opening Line, and each one can open the same body without contradiction.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the three hooks are recognisably aimed at three different states of
  awareness (problem-unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware) or three different buying objections,
  so the hook test is a real test. The strongest hook is not obvious on paper, which is the point.

---

## 6. Mechanism Clarity (pass >= 7)

**What it measures:** whether a viewer can say, in one sentence and in their own words, how the
product produced the result, in terms of this use case.

**Automatic ceiling:** the mechanism sentence is identical or near-identical across two scripts in
the batch, both capped at 5 (and it also costs parameter 4).

- **1-3:** No mechanism at all; the product appears and results follow. Or pure black box ("the
  algorithm does the rest"), or unexplained jargon that the persona would never use.
- **4-6:** A mechanism is stated but it is the same generic line every script uses, or it explains
  the category rather than this use case, or it runs so long that it stalls the story. The viewer
  could repeat the words without knowing what happens.
- **7-8:** One or two lines, in plain speech, adapted to this persona's situation, naming what the
  product actually does with what input and to what end. It is placed immediately after the product
  introduction and it makes the proof numbers feel inevitable rather than lucky.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the mechanism reframes the persona's original assumption, so the
  explanation itself is persuasive. It answers the hook's root cause directly and it is phrased in a
  way the viewer could repeat to a colleague without the ad.

---

## 7. Number Specificity and Believability (pass >= 7)

**What it measures:** whether the numbers are concrete, internally consistent, plausible at this
persona's scale, and expressed in the unit the persona actually tracks.

**Automatic ceiling:** any round hype number presented as an outcome (10,000 followers, 500% growth,
"went viral"), capped at 3. Numbers that conflict between spoken line and Overlay, capped at 4.

- **1-3:** No numbers, or vague magnitude words ("tons of", "blew up", "way more"), or hype rounds
  with no timeframe and no unit. Nothing a client could substantiate.
- **4-6:** Numbers exist but only at the reach rung, or the magnitudes are wrong for the persona (a
  solo operator reporting thousands of inbound requests), or the arithmetic does not hold (an
  engagement number larger than the reach number, a conversion rate that would be a category
  record), or timeframes are missing so the number floats.
- **7-8:** Three rungs with stated timeframes, magnitudes that sit inside what a business of this
  size would see, and consistent arithmetic: engagement is a believable fraction of reach, outcome a
  believable fraction of engagement. Spoken numbers and Overlay numbers match exactly.
- **9-10:** As above, plus a before-number so the delta is legible, and the outcome expressed in the
  persona's own vocabulary (booked shoots, signed clients, launch-day orders, held consults) rather
  than in platform vocabulary. The numbers are unremarkable enough to be true and specific enough to
  be checkable.

**Evidence required:** write out the three rungs with their numbers and do the division. If the
implied rate is above what the category sees, say so and prescribe a lower number.

## 8. Compliance Safety (pass >= 7)

**What it measures:** whether every claim is substantiable and no phrasing implies purchased,
guaranteed or numerically promised results.

**Automatic ceiling:** any phrasing that reads as followers being bought or guaranteed ("get 10K
followers", "guaranteed growth", "we add X per month"), capped at 2 and the line must be rewritten
before the script advances at all. Missing FTC substantiation disclaimer at the top of the output,
capped at 5.

- **1-3:** Purchase or guarantee implication present. Or absolute outcome promises, or income claims
  presented as typical, or health or financial claims with no qualifier. Individual results framed
  as what any buyer will get.
- **4-6:** No hard violation, but the boilerplate is doing the work: the trust line is spoken
  verbatim in every script, or the safe wording is applied so heavily that the copy goes limp, or
  the results framing is ambiguous about whether one person's outcome is being generalised.
- **7-8:** Compliance-safe vocabulary throughout (community, real people, discovery, reach) with the
  trust line delivered as an Overlay ("Overlay: No bots. No password needed.") rather than spoken in
  every script, and placed where the doubt actually lands rather than by rote. Substantiation
  disclaimer at the top of the output. Individual results are clearly individual.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the safe framing is more persuasive than the unsafe version would have
  been: "real people who were already looking" beats a follower count, and the trust Overlay lands
  as a specific objection killer at the exact beat a skeptic would push back.

---

## 9. Visual Proof Integration (pass >= 7)

**What it measures:** whether the inline Visual Cue and Overlay markers carry the persuasion, and
whether they are correct for the format the concept specifies.

Format rules that bind this score. Screen Share: the on-screen numbers are the argument, so the
cues must name what is on screen and the spoken line must not simply read the numbers aloud.
Comment Response: the comment itself has to carry enough context to work as the hook with sound off,
so it must be quoted in full in the cue.

**Automatic ceiling:** visual cues are decorative or generic ("[Visual Cue: creator smiling at
camera]") through the proof beat, capped at 4. Screen Share script with no numbers described on
screen, capped at 3. Comment Response script whose quoted comment does not stand alone, capped at 4.

- **1-3:** Cues describe mood, setting or B-roll and nothing else. Turn the sound off and no argument
  survives. Or cues are absent from the proof beat entirely.
- **4-6:** Cues exist and are relevant but they illustrate rather than prove: the product is shown,
  the dashboard is "shown" without saying what it reads, the numbers live only in the voiceover.
  Overlays repeat the spoken line word for word instead of adding the evidence.
- **7-8:** Every proof rung has a cue that names the specific artefact and the specific figure on it
  (the inbox count, the calendar with booked slots, the order notifications, the exact metric panel).
  Overlays add information the voiceover does not state. Sound off, the ad still argues.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the visual sequence is itself a ladder, escalating in the same order as
  the proof, and the format's native strength is exploited (the screen recording's raw look as
  credibility, the comment as a real objection). The spoken line and the on-screen evidence do
  different jobs and neither is redundant.

---

## 10. CTA Closing Strength (pass >= 7)

**What it measures:** whether the CTA closes the specific argument this concept made, then broadens
to the wider audience, with the offer restated on the end card.

**Automatic ceiling:** generic close with no tie to this concept ("Try it today", "Link in bio"),
capped at 4. No CTA Overlay end card where the format calls for one, capped at 5.

- **1-3:** A category CTA that could end any ad in the batch. No reference to the promise, no offer
  terms, no audience callout.
- **4-6:** The CTA references the product and the offer but not this script's argument, or it closes
  the argument and forgets to broaden, so it speaks only to the one persona in the story. The end
  card exists but restates the offer in the same words as another script.
- **7-8:** Two moves in order: close the exact argument this concept opened ("if your launch day
  went quiet, this is the fix"), then broaden the callout to the adjacent audiences the concept also
  serves. CTA Overlay end card repeats the offer terms in this script's own phrasing.
- **9-10:** As above, plus the close reuses the hook's own language so the ad snaps shut, the broaden
  move names concrete adjacent personas rather than "anyone who", and the end card gives one
  unambiguous next action with the offer terms attached.

---

## Scoring procedure

Follow these steps in order. Do not skip step 1, and do not start writing fixes before every
parameter has a number.

1. **Reset your position.** Score as a strategist who did not write the draft and has no stake in
   it. If you produced the script in this same run, state that in your notes and hold yourself to
   the stricter reading of every anchor. Where you are between two bands, take the lower one.
2. **Re-read the concept contract line before the script.** The contract is the standard. A
   beautifully written script that proves something the contract did not promise is a fail on
   parameter 1, not a near miss.
3. **Score the eight per-script parameters cold, in order, one pass each.** For parameters 1, 2, 3
   and 7 do the mechanical check described under the parameter (position fraction, proof ladder
   arithmetic, side-by-side introductions) rather than judging by feel.
4. **Score parameters 4 and 3's cross-check at batch level.** Run the swap test on every pair, list
   the pairs, and collect the product-introduction sentences and offer phrasings for the whole batch
   in one place. Duplicates found here also pull down the individual scripts that share them.
5. **Quote the failing line.** Every score below threshold must carry the exact line from the script,
   copied verbatim, with its location (hook number, sentence number or beat name). "The proof is
   weak" is not a finding. The four >= 8 parameters must carry their quoted evidence even when they
   pass, so the pass is auditable.
6. **Prescribe the fix, do not describe the problem.** Each finding gets a one-line instruction
   specific enough to execute without further thought: which beat to move, which unit to change the
   proof to, which construction to replace the introduction with, which sentence the offer moves
   into. Where a number is implausible, name the number to use instead.
7. **Revise and re-score. Maximum 2 revision cycles.** Apply the prescriptions, then re-run the full
   scorecard, not just the failing rows, because moving the offer or the proof usually shifts
   parameters 3, 5, 9 and 10 as well. Two cycles is the hard ceiling.
8. **If it still fails after cycle 2, deliver flagged. Never ship silently.** Attach a short note at
   the top of the delivery naming the parameter, its score, its threshold, the quoted line and the
   prescribed fix that was not achievable, plus what is needed to close it (usually a client fact:
   a real metric, a substantiable outcome, a confirmed offer). A flagged delivery is an acceptable
   outcome. A quietly shipped 5 is not.

Flag note template:

```
REVIEW FLAG (delivered after 2 revision cycles)
Parameter: Proof Matching Narrative Promise (scored 6, threshold 8)
Script: 004_V1
Line: "and by the end of the week my notifications were still going"
Issue: contract promised booked shoots; proof stops at engagement.
Needed to close: one real booked-shoots figure for an off-season week, plus the
prior off-season baseline. Placeholder used in this draft.
```

## Worked example

Batch of three scripts for a discovery tool sold to solo service providers. This is the review of
script 004, the third in the batch. Names and figures are illustrative.

Contract line: "This ad tests whether the product can keep a photographer booked off-season, proven
by weekly inquiries to booked shoots."

File-name line under review: `Brand_Video_9x16_30s_ScreenShare_004_V1_Batch3_EN`

### Cycle 1

| # | Parameter | Pass | Score | Verdict | Quoted evidence and prescribed fix |
|---|-----------|------|-------|---------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 | Proof Matching Narrative Promise | >= 8 | 5 | FAIL | "My DMs finally had people in them." Engagement standing in for the contract's booked shoots. Fix: end the ladder on booked shoots for a named off-season week, with the prior year's number as the baseline. |
| 2 | Offer Placement | >= 8 | 4 | FAIL | "It's 50% off right now if you want to try it." Sentence 13 of 14, bolted on. Fix: move the terms into the adoption beat as a first-person action, and restate them on the CTA Overlay. |
| 3 | Product Introduction | >= 8 | 5 | FAIL | "That's where it came in." Also used in script 002. Fix: rebuild as a second discovery channel found after referrals dried up, in the speaker's voice. |
| 4 | Differentiation Across Concepts | >= 8 | 6 | FAIL | Scripts 002 and 004 swap middles undetectably: same mechanism line, same spoken trust line, both proofs end on DMs. Fix: rewrite 004's mechanism around off-season timing and move its trust line to an Overlay at the skeptic beat. |
| 5 | Hook Distinctness | >= 7 | 7 | PASS | Hook 1: Dead January / Pain Point. Hook 2: Fully Booked Summer, Empty Winter / Goal. Hook 3: Wrong Audience / Root Cause. Three entry points, labelled, each opens the body cleanly. |
| 6 | Mechanism Clarity | >= 7 | 5 | FAIL | "It finds the people who are actually looking." Identical to script 002 and not adapted to off-season. Fix: one line on surfacing people planning shoots months ahead, so winter outreach lands before the booking decision. |
| 7 | Number Specificity and Believability | >= 7 | 6 | FAIL | "Thousands of people saw it and my DMs went crazy." No timeframe, no arithmetic, top rung missing. Fix: 4,100 reach in one week, 38 profile taps, 9 inquiries, 3 booked shoots, against 0 booked the previous January. |
| 8 | Compliance Safety | >= 7 | 7 | PASS | No purchase or guarantee implication. "Real people who were already planning shoots" is safe and specific. Disclaimer present at top. Trust line is spoken, which costs parameter 4 rather than this one. |
| 9 | Visual Proof Integration | >= 7 | 4 | FAIL | "*[Visual Cue: photographer scrolling her phone, smiling]*" through the proof beat, on a Screen Share format with no numbers described on screen. Fix: cue the metric panel with the reach figure, then the inquiry thread, then the calendar with three booked dates. |
| 10 | CTA Closing Strength | >= 7 | 5 | FAIL | "Give it a shot." No argument close, no broaden, no offer on the end card. Fix: close on the dead-January promise, broaden to other seasonal service businesses, add CTA Overlay with the annual-plan terms. |

Cycle 1 result: 8 of 10 failing. Prescriptions applied.

|---|-----------|------|-------|---------|--------------|

| 2 | Offer Placement | >= 8 | 8 | PASS | "I took the annual plan while it was half off, because I only get one off-season to fix." Sentence 5 of 15, inside the adoption beat, restated on the end card. |
| 3 | Product Introduction | >= 8 | 8 | PASS | "Referrals had carried me for two summers and they did nothing in December, so I went looking for a second way to be found." Unique in the batch. |
| 4 | Differentiation Across Concepts | >= 8 | 8 | PASS | Swap test now fails on every pair: 004's mechanism is timing-based, 002's is audience-based, 003's is objection-based; three different proof units; three offer phrasings. |
| 5 | Hook Distinctness | >= 7 | 8 | PASS | Unchanged, and the three hooks now map to three awareness states. |
| 6 | Mechanism Clarity | >= 7 | 8 | PASS | "It surfaces people who are planning shoots months out, so I am in front of them before they start asking around." |

| 8 | Compliance Safety | >= 7 | 9 | PASS | Trust line moved to "Overlay: Real people. No bots. No password needed." at the skeptic beat, no longer spoken in all three scripts. |
| 9 | Visual Proof Integration | >= 7 | 8 | PASS | Three cues escalate with the ladder: metric panel, inquiry thread, calendar with three booked dates. Argument survives with sound off. |

Cycle 2 result: all 10 pass. Batch ships. No flag needed.

Had parameter 1 stalled at 6 because no real booked-shoots figure existed, the correct action would
have been to deliver with the flag note shown above, not to substitute an engagement metric and let
the row read as a pass.

## Reviewer quick reference

- Contract line read before the script, every time.
- Four parameters at >= 8, six at >= 7, every script on its own row, no averaging past a gate.
- Automatic ceilings apply before judgement: bought or guaranteed followers, offer in the last
  sentence, shared product-introduction construction, wrong proof unit, decorative visuals on a
  Screen Share, generic CTA.
- Every sub-threshold score carries a verbatim quoted line and an executable fix.
- The four >= 8 parameters carry quoted evidence even when they pass.
- Batch-level swap test run on every pair, pairs listed.
- Two revision cycles maximum, full re-score each cycle.
- Still failing after cycle 2: deliver with the flag note. Never ship a quiet failure.
- No em dashes anywhere in the scripts or in the review notes.
