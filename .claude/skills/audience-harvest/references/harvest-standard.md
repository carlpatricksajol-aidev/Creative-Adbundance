# Harvest Standard

How to judge a harvested quote, and how to turn a surviving quote into an observation without
laundering it into strategy language.

This file is the quality gate for the audience-harvest skill. The skill's job is to find what a
brand's customers actually say in public, in their own words, and hand the ad-concept-generator a
list of SOURCED observations. Every observation carries the URL it came from. Nothing invented may
pass as harvested. If you are unsure whether something survives, this file decides.

## The standard, restated

An observation is:

"a specific behavior, thought, situation, conversation, or internet habit someone in the ICP
would recognise in one second."

Not a benefit. Not an angle. Not a theme.

    Bad: "mental load."      Good: "standing in front of the fridge trying to remember what I need to do tomorrow."
    Bad: "affordability."    Good: "checked my bank account after the pharmacy, then again to make sure the number was right."
    Bad: "convenience."      Good: "vacation in seven days and just realized I'm out."
    Bad: "personalization."  Good: "if HRT providers had dating profiles..."

Read those four pairs before every harvest. The left column is what a strategist writes when they
have not done the work. The right column is what a person actually did, at a specific hour, in a
specific room, with a specific object in their hand. The whole skill is the distance between the
two columns.

The one-second test is literal. Read the observation aloud to someone in the ICP. If they need a
beat to work out what you mean, it failed. If they say "that's me" before you finish the sentence,
it passed.

## The KEEP / CUT test

Run every candidate quote through five questions in this order. The first NO is a CUT.

1. Is there a MOMENT in it? A time, a place, a physical object, an action, a person on the other
   end of a sentence. If you cannot draw it, there is no moment.
2. Is it SPECIFIC to a life, not to a category? "I care about X" is a category. "I hid the box in
   the garage" is a life.
3. Would a stranger in the same ICP RECOGNISE it in one second, without explanation?
4. Is a real person SAYING it, in first person, in a place I can link to?
5. Could we build an ad around it WITHOUT making a claim we are not allowed to make?

A KEEP has five yeses. Everything else is a CUT, and the CUT goes in your notes but not in the
deliverable.

The examples below are invented for teaching. They are written the way real posts read, typos and
run-ons included, because that is what you will actually be looking at. The brand names in them are
invented too.

### Example 1: the review rather than a moment

Quote: "Great product, fast shipping, will definitely order again. 5 stars."

Verdict: CUT. There is no moment in it, only a verdict about a transaction, and a verdict is what a
person writes when they have nothing to report.

### Example 2: the benefit restated

Quote: "Honestly it saves me so much time. Such a time saver. Worth every penny."

Verdict: CUT. This is the benefit our own marketing already claims, handed back to us in the
customer's mouth, which teaches the concept generator nothing that was not already on the brief.

### Example 3: the category abstraction

Quote: "I really care about sustainability and I try to buy from brands that share my values."

Verdict: CUT. "Sustainability" and "values" are the labels a person reaches for when asked to
summarise themselves, and a label is the opposite of a moment.

### Example 4: the one-off so idiosyncratic nobody recognises it

Quote: "Used it to clean the grout behind my late uncle's aquarium stand before the estate sale on
the 14th, which was the only day my cousin could drive up from Bakersfield."

Verdict: CUT. Every detail is load-bearing to exactly one person, so nobody else sees themselves in
it, and specificity nobody shares is just noise.

### Example 5: the ad or paid post

Quote: "Obsessed with my new @NorthlaneGoods bundle! Use code SARAH20 for 20% off, link in bio.
#gifted #ad"

Verdict: CUT. A paid post is our own category talking to itself, and quoting it back is the purest
form of hearing our own echo and calling it research.

Tells: a discount code, "gifted", "partner", "#ad", a perfectly on-message sentence with no
friction in it, or an account whose last thirty posts are all different brands.

### Example 6: the question with no situation in it

Quote: "Does anyone know if this is any good? Thinking about trying it."

Verdict: CUT. A question can be an observation, but only when the situation that forced it is
inside it, and here there is no situation, only an intent to purchase. Compare Example 10, the same
shape, which survives because the asker brought her kitchen with her.

### Example 7: the secondhand summary from an article

Quote: "According to a recent analysis of Reddit discussions, consumers in this space are
increasingly frustrated with subscription fatigue and want more transparency."

Verdict: CUT. This is a writer summarising people, not a person speaking, and articles ABOUT what a
forum says are explicitly not customer voice.

This is the most common way a harvest gets contaminated, because these articles rank well and read
fluently. A marketing blog paraphrasing a thread is not a source. Find the thread.

### Example 8: the quote so old the behaviour has moved on

Quote (posted 2017): "I always keep the paper insert from the box in my wallet so I remember the
reorder number when I'm at the store."

Verdict: CUT. The behaviour depends on a world where you reorder in a physical store from a paper
insert, and the same person in 2026 taps a button, so the moment no longer describes anyone.

Rule of thumb: anything older than about three years needs a second look, and anything whose
mechanics depend on a discontinued app, a dead platform feature, or a pre-pandemic routine is out
however good the sentence is. Record the post date on every candidate as you collect it, so this
test is a check rather than a guess at review time.

### Example 9: the quote carrying a claim we could not repeat

Quote: "Three weeks on this and my A1C dropped two points and I got off my blood pressure meds
completely. My doctor was shocked."

Verdict: CUT for the deliverable, FLAG for the strategist. The moment is vivid and real, but it is
a medical outcome claim, and an observation that cannot survive into an ad without becoming a
regulatory problem is a trap laid for the creative team downstream.

Do not quietly soften it to "my numbers looked better" and pass it as harvested, because that is
inventing a quote. Either cut it, or keep a surrounding situation that carries no claim (for
example "sat in the parking lot reading the printout on my phone before driving home") and cut the
outcome. That surrounding situation must be something the person actually described, not a scene
you imagined around their claim.

### Example 10: the question that DOES carry a situation

Quote: "how do people do this with a toddler?? im standing in the kitchen at 6am with one shoe on
trying to read the instructions on the back while she pulls on my leg"

Verdict: KEEP. The question is only the wrapper, and inside it are a kitchen, an hour, one shoe, a
label, and a child pulling on a leg.

Observation text: "6am in the kitchen with one shoe on, reading the back of the box while a toddler
pulls on your leg."

### Example 11: the strong keep that needs only tightening

Quote: "ok so its 11pm and im standing in the bathroom doing the thing where i open the cabinet,
look at the same four bottles, close it, and open it again like something new is going to be in
there lol"

Verdict: KEEP. A room, an hour, a physical action, and a self-aware joke about the action, which is
exactly the shape of a scroll-stopping hook. It needs trimming, not rebuilding.

Observation text: "11pm, opening the bathroom cabinet, looking at the same four bottles, closing
it, opening it again like something new will be in there."

### Example 12: the confession

Quote: "i told my sister i'd 'been meaning to sort it out' which is a lie, i have a note in my
phone from february that just says CALL THEM"

Verdict: KEEP. This answers two harvest-bank prompts at once, what they lied about to seem more
together and the Notes app open on their phone, and the specific dead note is what makes it land.

Observation text: "Telling your sister you've 'been meaning to sort it out', with a note in your
phone from February that just says CALL THEM."

### Example 13: the discovery moment

Quote: "logged in to cancel and saw i'd been charged since march. march!! i genuinely thought i
cancelled this in like, one sitting on a sunday"

Verdict: KEEP. The subscription they forgot they had, with the beat that makes it usable, which is
not the money but the discovery of how long it has been running.

Observation text: "Logging in to cancel and finding out you've been charged since March."

### Example 14: the comparison scroll

Quote: "the algorithm has decided i am a person with a routine and every single video is somebody
with 11 bottles on a marble tray and i have 2 and one of them is from a hotel"

Verdict: KEEP. The Instagram-scroll prompt answered with real numbers and a real object, and the
hotel bottle is the detail a copywriter could not have invented off a brief.

Observation text: "Every video on your feed is someone with eleven bottles on a marble tray. You
have two, and one of them is from a hotel."

### Example 15: the deferral

Quote: "signed up at like 11:47pm after saying 'ill figure it out later' for two months straight,
then immediately felt weird about it"

Verdict: KEEP. The 11:47 PM signup is straight off the harvest bank, and "immediately felt weird
about it" is the honest half most brands would cut, so keep the whole thing.

Observation text: "Signing up at 11:47pm after two months of saying 'I'll figure it out later',
then immediately feeling weird about it."

### Example 16: the near-miss that looks like a keep

Quote: "It's become part of my daily routine and I love the ritual of it. Self care matters."

Verdict: CUT. "Ritual" and "self care" are category words wearing a first-person costume, and there
is no hour, no room, and no object in the sentence.

### Example 17: the vivid quote about the wrong ICP

Quote: "as a warehouse supervisor i order forty of these a month and the pallets always show up
split"

Verdict: CUT for a DTC consumer brief, KEEP for a B2B or bulk brief. The moment is excellent and
completely real, but it belongs to a buyer the brief is not targeting, and a strong observation
aimed at the wrong person still burns a concept slot. Log it in the coverage note as an
adjacent-audience find rather than dropping it silently.

## Writing the text field

The `text` field is the observation as the concept generator will read it. Five rules.

1. Keep their register and their nouns. If they wrote "the cabinet", do not write "the medicine
   cabinet". If they wrote "the thing on my phone", do not write "the mobile app". Their noun is
   the evidence that a person said it.
2. Cut the throat-clearing. "ok so", "does anyone else", "sorry for the long post", "just me?",
   and every hedge that exists to soften a stranger's post. Cut hashtags, usernames, and platform
   furniture. Keep the words that carry the moment.
3. Never translate into strategy language. The moment you write "friction", "consideration stage",
   "trust barrier", or "mental load", you have thrown away the only thing you were sent to get.
   The concept generator does that translation itself, later, and it does it better with the raw
   material intact.
4. Never add a benefit that was not there. If they described a problem and stopped, the
   observation stops. Do not append "and that's why she switched." An unresolved moment is a hook,
   a resolved one is a testimonial, and we are not harvesting testimonials.
5. Keep it to the length of a sentence someone recognises in one second: one sentence, roughly
   eight to twenty-five words. Two short sentences only when the second is the turn (Example 14).
   If it needs a comma-spliced third clause to make sense, the moment is not sharp enough yet.

Punctuation: fix capitals and obvious typos so it reads cleanly, keep the speaker's contractions
and slang, and use plain ASCII with no em dashes and no curly quotes. You are transcribing tidily,
not rewriting.

### Five before/after rewrites

Before: "Honestly does anyone else do this or is it just me lol but I will genuinely put the box by
the front door for like four days before I actually take it to the post office. FOUR DAYS."

After: "The return box sitting by the front door for four days before it makes it to the post
office."

Changed: the hedge and the "just me" opener are gone. The number stayed, because the number is the
joke.

Before: "So my dentist asked me point blank at my cleaning last week whether I'd been using it
every night and I straight up lied to a medical professional lmao"

After: "Lying to your dentist about whether you've been using it every night."

Changed: the dialogue frame collapsed to the action, and "medical professional" was dropped in
favour of the specific person, because "dentist" is the noun that draws the room.

Before: "I think what it really comes down to for me is trust. Like I need to know a brand is being
straight with me about what's actually in there before I'm going to commit to a subscription."

After: CUT. Nothing survives.

Changed: nothing could. This is a category abstraction ("trust", "commit") with no moment
underneath. There is no honest rewrite. Writing "reading the ingredient list twice before
subscribing" would be inventing a scene the person never described.

Before: "3am, wide awake, phone at 12% brightness, googling the exact same question I googled last
month and getting the exact same four articles"

After: "3am, phone brightness at its lowest, googling the same question you googled last month and
getting the same four articles."

Changed: almost nothing, which is correct. A quote this well-formed gets tightened, not rebuilt.
"12%" became "its lowest" only because the exact number was doing less work than the behaviour.

Before: "yeah my group chat has now sent me the same tiktok about this four separate times and at
this point i feel like im being targeted by my own friends"

After: "The same TikTok landing in your group chat four separate times, until it starts to feel
like your friends are targeting you."

Changed: kept the joke and the count, straightened the syntax, resisted adding "so she finally
bought it."

## Insight families

The concept generator groups observations into insight families and caps a single batch at two
concepts per family. That cap only works if the families are drawn correctly.

Group by the UNDERLYING OBSERVATION, not by the situation and not by the vehicle.

- The SITUATION is where it happened: the kitchen, the parking lot, the group chat.
- The VEHICLE is the format a concept would use: the "put a finger down" trend, the POV, the
  listicle, the talking head.
- The UNDERLYING OBSERVATION is what is true about the person regardless of room or format.

Two observations belong in the same family when swapping the room or the format leaves them saying
the same thing about the person. If you can move one into the other's setting and it still reads as
a near-duplicate, it is one family.

### The trap

Four concepts about friction, set in a kitchen, a car, a bathroom, and an office, are ONE family in
four outfits. They will look like four ideas on a slide and behave like one idea in the auction,
because the audience does not experience them as different claims. Ship two at most and spend the
other slots on a family you actually found.

The trap runs the other way with vehicles. Four "put a finger down" concepts drawn from four
genuinely different underlying observations are FOUR families sharing a costume, and the cap does
not apply. The cap counts observations, not formats.

### Worked example: twelve observations into five families

Brand: an invented DTC refillable-cleaning company, "Halden Home". Twelve kept observations:

1. "Standing in the laundry room holding two nearly-empty bottles, deciding which one dies first."
2. "The cabinet under the sink where nothing stands upright any more."
3. "Buying the same spray at the store because you cannot remember whether you have one at home."
4. "Reordering at 11:40pm from bed because you noticed the last pod at dinner."
5. "Your mom asking why you don't just buy the big jug like a normal person."
6. "Explaining to your partner that no, the refill is not the same as the bottle."
7. "The delivery box you have not flattened, holding the door open."
8. "Keeping the empty bottle on the counter for a week as a reminder to order more."
9. "Getting the same cleaning TikTok forwarded by two different people in one day."
10. "Watching a video of someone's colour-matched pantry and looking at your own shelf."
11. "The subscription you forgot about arriving the week you finally bought a spare in person."
12. "Sitting on the floor pulling everything out from under the sink, then putting most of it back."

FAMILY A, Inventory blindness: you do not know what you already own. Members 1, 3, 8, 11.
Different rooms and hours, but every one is a person unable to see their own stock. Note that 11
could read as subscription mistrust; it is filed here because the failure is inventory, not billing.

FAMILY B, Under-sink archaeology: the storage itself is the problem. Members 2, 12. Both are about
physical accumulation and the shame of the space, not about running out.

FAMILY C, Defending the choice to someone older or closer. Members 5, 6. Both are dialogue moments
where the person has to justify the category to a skeptic. Different skeptics, same defence.

FAMILY D, The aesthetic gap on your feed. Members 9, 10. Both are comparison-by-algorithm, where
the pressure arrives through a screen rather than through a person.

FAMILY E, The residue of ordering: what the buying leaves behind. Members 4, 7. Both are about the
awkward edges of the delivery loop, one at the moment of ordering and one days later.

Yield: five families, so a batch can carry up to ten concepts without repeating itself, and the two
thinnest families (B and D, two members each) get flagged in the coverage note as places where more
harvesting would help.

What NOT to do: file 1, 2, 3 and 12 as "kitchen", "under the sink", "the store" and "the floor" and
call that four families. Those are four rooms and two ideas.

## Provenance and honesty

### Every observation carries its URL

Not the domain. Not "Reddit". Not "a forum thread". The permalink to the specific comment or post,
and where the platform supports it, the comment permalink rather than the thread root. Alongside
it, record the platform, the post date, and the quote as it actually appeared, before your tidy-up.
The `text` field is your edit. The raw quote is the receipt.

The client is paying for sourced customer voice. An observation without a working URL is not a
weaker observation, it is a different product, and shipping it inside the same list is a
misrepresentation.

### When a quote cannot be re-found

- You have the URL and the page is now dead: keep the observation, keep the dead URL, mark it
  `verification: dead-link` with the retrieval date. The client can see exactly what happened.
- You have a snippet but never had a URL: CUT IT. A quote you cannot point at is indistinguishable
  from a quote you wrote, which is precisely the failure this file exists to prevent.
- The quote came from a search result summary rather than the page: open the page. If it will not
  open (403 from a datacenter IP is common), you may keep the observation only if the search result
  shows the verbatim sentence, and you must record the search result as the retrieval path. Say so.
  Do not launder a snippet into a page read.
- Never reconstruct a quote from memory of having seen it. Reconstruction is invention.

### A short harvest beats a padded one

Fifteen real observations from four sources is a better deliverable than forty of which
twenty-five are reviews, paraphrases, and things you were fairly sure someone says. The cap of two
per family means a ten-concept batch needs about five good families, and five good families need
roughly fifteen to twenty-five strong observations. Above that you are producing volume for its own
sake.

Padding actively damages the downstream work, because a weak observation looks identical to a
strong one at generation time, and the concept built on it occupies a slot, gets built, gets shot,
and fails in the auction with no way to trace why.

If the harvest comes up short, the correct output is a short harvest with an honest coverage note.
Say "this brand's customers are not discussing this publicly in volume" out loud. That is a real
finding a strategist can act on. Filling the gap with plausible sentences is the one unrecoverable
failure.

### The specific failure to watch for in yourself

Stated bluntly, because it is the one that will actually happen.

A model doing this work will at some point produce a sentence that sounds exactly like a real post.
It will have the lowercase, the "lol", the specific hour, the physical object. It will feel
harvested, because it is built out of the pattern of a thousand real posts. And it will not exist.
Nobody wrote it. There is no URL, or worse, there is a plausible-looking URL that was also
generated.

This is not lying in the ordinary sense. It is the pattern completing itself and the output feeling
retrieved rather than composed, which is exactly why it is dangerous: it does not feel like
fabrication from the inside. The confidence is identical. If it reaches the client, the agency has
told a paying customer that their audience said something their audience never said, and concepts
get shot on the strength of it.

Self-check, run on every observation before it goes in the file:

1. Can I name the exact page this came from, and did I open it or see the verbatim text in a search
   result in this session? If the answer contains "I believe" or "it was something like", it is
   invented. Cut it.
2. Is the URL in my notes one I retrieved, or one I produced? A retrieved permalink carries a post
   ID and a comment ID you did not choose. If you cannot say where the ID came from, cut it.
3. Can I quote the surrounding context? Real sources have a thread title, a subforum, a date, other
   replies. If the quote floats with nothing around it, you did not read a page. Cut it.
4. Scan the list for uniformity. Real harvested quotes are uneven: some run two words long, some
   have a weird tangent, some are inconsistently punctuated. If every entry is a well-formed,
   similarly-sized, similarly-witty sentence, the list was smoothed or composed. Go back to the raw
   quotes.
5. Confirm the count. Observations must be fewer than or equal to the raw quotes you actually
   collected. If observations outnumber collected quotes, something was generated. Cheapest check,
   catches the most.

When in doubt, cut. A cut observation costs one slot. An invented one costs the client's trust and
the agency's claim to be doing research at all.

### Access reality, so nobody wastes an hour

Measured 2026-09-04. Do not contradict this in your notes or your plan.

- Reddit anonymous .json: 403 from datacenter IPs, confirmed by direct test. Official API: new app
  registrations manually reviewed and largely refused since 2025-11, so never advise registering
  one. Anthropic's crawler is blocked by reddit.com, so domain-filtered web search and direct fetch
  both refuse. Working access needs a signed-in desktop browser session: a CLI reusing Chrome
  cookies, or a cookie pasted into a CLI.
- PullPush, the Pushshift successor: 429 on repeated tries from a datacenter IP.
- Twitter/X: v2 API returns 401 without paid auth. Practical access is auth_token and ct0 cookies
  from a signed-in browser.
- Trustpilot: 403 to a plain request. Styleforum: 403 to a plain request from a datacenter IP, but
  reachable via web search results.
- Hacker News Algolia API: 200, free, no key, confirmed working. General web search works and does
  surface forum threads and review pages.
- An LLM web-search plugin tested for verbatim Reddit quotes returned only marketing articles ABOUT
  Reddit at roughly $0.26 per query. Those are not customer voice (see Example 7).

Practical consequence: when Reddit is unreachable in a run, say so in the coverage note and harvest
elsewhere. Do not substitute articles about Reddit. Do not paraphrase what you expect it would say.

## Coverage note template

Every harvest ships with this block, filled in honestly. It is the second half of the deliverable
and it is what makes a short harvest defensible.

```
COVERAGE NOTE
Brand: <brand>
Brief / ICP: <one line, as given>
Harvest date: <YYYY-MM-DD>

SOURCES SEARCHED
- <platform or site> | <access path: web search / Algolia API / signed-in session / direct fetch>
  | <queries or subforums used> | <pages opened> | <quotes collected>

SOURCES SKIPPED AND WHY
- <platform> | <reason, from the access reality list or a run-specific failure>
  e.g. Reddit | 403 on the .json endpoint from this IP and crawler blocked; no signed-in session
  available this run.
  e.g. Trustpilot | 403 to plain request; no verbatim customer sentences retrievable via search.

COUNTS
Quotes collected (raw, with URLs): <n>
Quotes kept as observations: <n>
Cut, by reason: reviews <n> / benefit restatements <n> / category abstractions <n> /
  ads or paid posts <n> / secondhand summaries <n> / too old <n> / unusable claims <n> /
  wrong ICP <n> / no situation <n> / unverifiable <n>

FAMILIES FOUND
- <Family name>: <n> observations. <one line on what is true about the person>
Concept ceiling implied by the cap of two per family: <2 x number of families>

WHAT IS THIN
- <Family> has only <n> observations and should not carry two concepts.
- <Audience segment named in the brief> produced zero usable quotes; they may not discuss this
  publicly, or they may live on a platform we could not reach this run.
- <Anything you looked for and did not find, stated plainly.>

ADJACENT FINDS (not in the deliverable list)
- <strong observations belonging to a different buyer than the brief's, kept for the record>

VERIFICATION FLAGS
- <n> observations carry verification: dead-link, listed with retrieval dates.
- <n> observations were verified from a search-result snippet rather than an opened page.
```

Fill in the zeros. A coverage note with a blank "what is thin" section on a run where Reddit was
unreachable is not an honest note. The thin sections are the ones the strategist reads first.
