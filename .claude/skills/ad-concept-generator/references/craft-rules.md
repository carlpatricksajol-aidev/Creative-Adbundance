# Craft rules — writing the concepts

Hard-won rules from real client feedback (the telehealth account, a social-growth tool, a social-growth tool, the parenting app). Every rule below
exists because a concept failed a specific way in front of a client. Read them literally.

## The generation formula (run per concept, in order)

1. **Performance filter.** Generate only from the approved winner set for this brand (angles,
   formats). Untested formats run as a small controlled test — never the bulk of the batch.
2. **Pick a specific observation from the harvest.** Not a benefit. Not an angle. An observation. A
   behavior, thought, situation, conversation, or internet habit the ICP would recognize in one
   second. Examples: "vacation in seven days, out of BC" / "pediatrician asked me a question I
   couldn't remember the answer to" / "my bank account is confused" / "standing in front of the
   fridge trying to remember what I need to do tomorrow" / "if HRT providers had dating profiles."
   Pull from `libraries.md` harvest bank; extend it.
3. **Make one creative leap.** Do NOT dramatize the observation directly. Find an unexpected but
   immediately understandable device that expresses it. Insight → cultural/social behavior →
   creative device → brand. See the vehicle library in `libraries.md`.
4. **Assign one vehicle.** The vehicle IS the ad — dating profile, banking-app POV, group chat,
   side quest, "did you know?" fake explainer, comment reply, ranking video, mock documentary,
   confessional, GRWM, receipt printer, wall calendar diary, "put a finger down," "green flags of
   [x]," etc. If the concept doesn't have a specific vehicle, it's not a concept — it's an angle.
5. **Pick one persuasion job.** One objection this ad handles (price, personalization, convenience,
   symptom relief, trust, options, safety, effectiveness...). Every other benefit sits down. If the
   ad is arguing three things, it argues none. This is the single hardest rule for the AI to obey.
6. **Assign a believable trigger.** Why is this person showing me this right now? A skeptical friend
   asked; a text came in; a "put a finger down" trend passed by; found video; brand shoot in an
   hour; a bill just landed. No believable trigger → the premise is rejected before it's written.
7. **Assign a visual proof object + proof behavior.** One visible object (calendar, DMs, receipt,
   pill pack, app screen, kitchen counter, empty box). The proof is **behaved** — sent, swiped,
   compared, reacted to, exposed, replayed, received, printed. Not just displayed.
8. **Brand slots into the vehicle — it doesn't star.** The product enables the human story; it
   doesn't take over. Vary how the product enters across the batch — early / late / visually /
   humorously / accidentally / by comparison. Never five concepts in a row where the product enters
   at beat 3 with an intake screen.
9. **Land the payoff on the outcome ladder.** End on a concrete, persona-specific outcome — an
   inquiry, a booking, a delivery, a filled program, an eye-roll, an "I did it" moment. Spread
   payoffs across the batch. A single outcome (a follower count, a delivery box) as the payoff of
   every concept means the batch has one payoff, not ten.
10. **Compare-before-output check.** Before finalizing, compare against the batch. If the consumer
    situation, visual experience, or storytelling mechanic feels substantially similar to something
    already there, discard and generate another. Changing the hook or benefit does NOT make it a new
    concept.

## Batch composition targets (survival analysis, the telehealth account's Batch 3)

When the client finalized the telehealth account's Batch 3, only 3 of 16 generated concepts survived. The replacements
shared patterns the generated batch lacked. These are now hard composition targets — check the
batch against ALL of them before the strategist gate:

1. **Differentiator-first (when a business objective exists).** If the brief states an objective
   ("communicate why [brand] beats other providers"), a meaningful share of the batch (~1/3) must
   be BUILT AROUND the differentiator — not the category. Vehicles that carry differentiation
   natively: tier lists ("provider support as your needs change → S, one-size-fits-all → D"),
   blind reviews ("Website A / B / C" with a pinned checklist, brand revealed last), side-by-side
   scorecards, "what actually matters when choosing" rankings. A batch that sells "start HRT"
   when the objective is "why the telehealth account's HRT" fails even if every concept is individually good.
2. **Insight-family cap.** Dedup one level deeper than situation/vehicle: group concepts by the
   INSIGHT FAMILY they express ("getting BC the old way is friction," "nighttime symptom
   desperation," "OTC skincare doesn't work"). Max ~2 concepts per family per batch. Four
   concepts on friction (moving, rationing, copay, pharmacy calls) are one insight in four
   outfits, even with four distinct vehicles. Two 3 A.M. concepts (search history + notes diary)
   are one concept.
3. **Research numbers quota.** If the research docs carry proof stats (94.6% saw improvement, 20X
   stronger, 81% collagen, 16 weeks), at least 2–3 concepts must be stat-led or stat-carrying —
   including graphic treatments (a 100-icon wall lighting up to 94.6%) and stat/price-bearing
   titles ("Watch My Wrinkles Transform in 3 Months for Under $40/Month," "100 Women Tried It").
   A batch with zero research numbers is leaving the client's strongest ammunition in the doc.
4. **Second-character quota.** At least 2–3 concepts per batch involve a second character or
   relationship dynamic: partner POV (husband watching menopause chaos), parent/child dual
   routine (daughter recommends to mom), roommate discovery, friend call-out — or the product
   itself personified as a character (birth control pack sitting across the desk for its annual
   performance review). All-solo batches read flat and cast identically.
5. **Production-lane spread.** Not everything is a live creator. Include 1–3 graphic-led or
   animated concepts (stat walls, price-comparison graphics, Wrapped-style stat cards) when the
   client produces them. Tag every concept with a lane: Creator UGC / B-roll only / Animation /
   AI + B-roll / In-house. The client plans production off these tags.
6. **Trend-as-delivery-system quota.** 1–2 concepts per batch ride a recognizable cultural
   template where the trend IS the vehicle: "Your [X] Wrapped" year-in-review, annual performance
   review, tier list, blind taste-test/review, awards show, "put a finger down." Not a "trend
   bucket" at the end of the deck — the message rides inside the template.
7. **Tonal spread.** No more than ~half the batch in the same emotional register. Heavy categories
   (menopause, health anxiety) especially need comedy: the finals that replaced an earnest batch
   were husband-POV comedy, "Money spent: we're not discussing it" stat cards, and skits. Earnest
   + funny + deadpan + warm should all appear.
8. **Awareness-stage tag + spread.** Tag every concept Problem Aware / Solution Aware / Most
   Aware, and spread the batch across stages. Problem Aware: names the symptom/situation, brand
   arrives as discovery. Solution Aware: viewer knows solutions exist; the ad differentiates or
   handles an objection. Most Aware: offer/proof-led. The tag prints with the concept.

## Title — must name or imply the vehicle

The title is the fastest test of whether a concept is a concept.

- **Passes:** "HRT Dating Profile" · "Vacation Countdown" · "My Bank Account Is Confused" · "Put A
  Finger Down: Hormone Edition" · "Birth Control Side Quest" · "My Mom's HRT Advice vs. My
  Provider's" · "Group Chat Blowing Up About the telehealth account" · "Toxic Trait: I Forget My Pills" · "Did You
  Know: Skipping Periods" · "Search History: 3 A.M."
- **Fails:** "I Became My Own Doctor. Then I Hired One." (line, not vehicle) · "The 2.4-Year
  Reckoning" (headline, not vehicle) · "Same Diet, Different Middle" (message, no vehicle) · "The
  Reason I Actually Clicked" (angle, not vehicle).

Test: can a reader picture the ad from the title alone? If not, the title is wrong. A strong line
that isn't a vehicle can move to the description as the emotional hook — it doesn't belong in the
title.

Three title patterns the client's final decks favor, beyond naming the vehicle:
- **Stat/price in the title** when the number is the hook: "Watch My Wrinkles Transform in 3
  Months for Under $40/Month" · "100 Women Tried It."
- **Question titles** that qualify the viewer: "Are You Using a Retinoid at Night?" ·
  "Prescription Skincare Is Expensive… Right?"
- **Trend-template titles** where the template is instantly recognizable: "Your Skincare Wrapped"
  · "My Birth Control Has a Performance Review."

## Description — what we're making (not why it works)

2-3 sentences of plain 5th-grade language. Answer these questions and stop:

- **What creator format is it?** (car talking head, GRWM, comment reply, mock dating profile, etc.)
- **What's the situation/scene?** (bathroom mirror, moving-day kitchen, vacation packing, parked
  car outside the OB)
- **What's the ONE core message?** (this one persuasion job — not five)
- **How does the brand fit in?** (early / late / by accident / as the punchline)
- **What's the one idea running through the whole ad?**

Cut anything that sounds like an internal strategy memo. Any of these phrases = automatic rewrite:
"positions the app as...", "reframes the problem...", "dramatises the mental load...", "acts as a
single source of truth...", "quietly powers that confidence...", "lands the [x] benefit...",
"proves the value...", "gives Meta a distinct creative signal...", "the identity lane...", "the
$XX CPA winner category...".

**Meta-narration ban (the disguised version of strategist-language).** Sentences that NAME the
device instead of showing it are the same failure in different clothes. Automatic rewrite:
"the ranking IS the vehicle" · "the [x] IS the ad" · "the format IS the vehicle" · "the
casualness is the proof" · "the continuity is the whole message" · "the friction stack is the
vehicle" · "the confession format IS the vehicle" · "the receipts do the arguing" · "the math
does the talking" · "the overcompensation IS the ad" · "identity-led" · "[x]-led" · "the
simplicity IS the flex". The device must be evident from plainly describing what happens on
screen. Test: delete the meta-sentence. If the description still conveys the whole idea, the
sentence was dead weight — leave it out. If the idea collapses without it, the description
failed and needs rewriting — the meta-sentence was doing work the description should do.
Compare: "The whole video is the group chat playing out on screen" (describes what we see — OK)
vs. "The group chat IS the vehicle" (names the mechanism — cut).

Someone should understand the whole idea after reading the description once. If they need to
translate strategist jargon, it's failed.

- **Pass:** "A car talking-head from a mom on her lunch break about the pediatrician question she
  couldn't answer this morning. She opens the parenting app in the parking lot and finds the answer in
  three taps. The whole video is her one-take confession + one look at her phone."
- **Fail:** "A concept that positions the parenting app as a memory bank for mental-load carriers,
  reframing pediatrician anxiety into confident retrieval and proving the value of a single
  source of truth."

Same idea. The pass version is what we're actually making.

## Narrative — short shootable beatboard

**EXACTLY 3 bullets (v6).** Each bullet tells the creator what happens next in plain, action-based
language. Not a script. Not strategy notes. Beats, like a producer's shot list on a napkin.

v4 and v5 asked for 5-6 beats. v6 compresses to three, and each bullet may carry more than one
beat: (1) establish the situation / problem / premise, (2) introduce the product, mechanism or
proof, (3) deliver the payoff and turn toward the CTA. The compression is deliberate. It forces
the DR spine to be visible on the slide instead of the setup eating the whole beatboard. The
craft rules below are unchanged; only the count moved.

- **Do:** "Open in the car, seatbelt on, sunglasses off. She's on lunch." / "She opens the app on
  her phone. Real UI on-screen for 2 seconds." / "She reads the answer aloud, half-laughing."
- **Don't:** Full VO scripts. Long emotional interpretations. Multiple product beats stacked. Every
  beat starting with "she then..." like a template.

Vary the arc across the batch:
- **Vary how the product enters** — early / late / visually / humorously / accidentally / as the
  punchline / never (in favor of a graphic).
- **Vary how the concept ends** — a line, an action, a beat of silence, a delivery notification,
  the phone hitting the counter, the screen going black. Ending on "Close on [x]. CTA." across
  the batch is a template — CTA copy belongs to the script phase anyway, so most narratives
  shouldn't even mention it. No more than ~2 concepts in a batch may share the same close type.
- **Vary the opening device** — a line of dialogue, a buzzing phone, an image on screen, a scroll,
  a doorbell, a full body sitting on a couch not saying anything for a beat.
- **No recurring stock beat across the batch.** If the same beat type appears in most concepts —
  a "Quick overlay: '[benefits]'" beat, a "screen record of the intake" beat, a "40% off card"
  beat — it's a template even when everything around it varies. A beat type may repeat only where
  it's that concept's actual device.
- **Never five concepts in a row with the same shape** (open on person + name the problem + show
  the app + close on the improvement). If the skeletons match with nouns swapped, rewrite until
  they don't.
- **The slide serves the concept, not the concept the slide.** The count is fixed at 3, but beat
  density, where dialogue lives, and whether there's a price/offer beat all follow what THIS
  concept needs. A single-take confessional may be three long bullets; a fast ranking bit three
  clipped ones. If every narrative in the batch reads as three bullets of identical weight and
  shape, the format is driving the ideas.

One explicit product beat. The payoff must be persona-specific — never generic "she felt better"
or "numbers went up."

## Design Components — only what's unique + Duration

Assume standard UGC production. **Cut boilerplate.** Do not repeat these across slides: "native
captions," "quick cuts," "realistic app screens," "screen recording," "iPhone-native compression,"
"warm lighting," "natural sound." An editor already knows.

Include ONLY:
- The signature device unique to this concept (post-it wall, receipt printer, split-screen with a
  specific mechanic, wall calendar, phone-in-mirror selfie strip, dating-profile card layout).
- The editing rhythm if it matters (hard-cut list, real-time single take, day-part timelapse).
- Any prop or setup that isn't obvious from the narrative.
- Final line: `Duration: X seconds.`

**Consistency rule.** Every device in Design Components must already appear in the description or
narrative. If it doesn't, either add it to those sections or cut it from design. Design should
support what's already established — never introduce a new concept.

## One persuasion job per concept

The most-broken rule. Every concept picks ONE objection to answer:

- Price · Personalization · Convenience · Symptom relief · Trust · Options · Safety · Effectiveness
  · Continuity · Time · Guarantee · Speed · Options for X audience...

Every OTHER benefit stays out of the description and narrative. If the ad is arguing for price and
personalization and options and time and trust, it argues for nothing. Pick the objection, then
make the brand's corresponding advantage the hero. The other benefits will be tested in other
concepts.

## Five-dimension diversity (batch-level)

Real diversity means variation on multiple dimensions simultaneously:

**Angle × Human situation × Format × Visual device × Funnel job.**

Five talking-heads about five different benefits are **five angles but one concept**. A real batch
looks like:

- Refill anxiety × vacation × countdown POV × packing b-roll × acquisition
- Affordability × financial shock × banking-app comparison × receipts overlay × acquisition
- Convenience × forgetfulness × "toxic trait" TikTok confession × phone drop × retention/upsell
- Secondary benefits × discovery × "Did You Know?" fake explainer × card cutaways × education
- Choice overload × friend advice × group-chat chaos × text bubbles × consideration
- Access × pharmacy frustration × side-quest video-game POV × sound-effect punches × acquisition
- Trust × questions × comment-reply format × greenscreen react × trust-building
- Product selection × lifestyle × dating-profile POV × swipe mechanic × top-of-funnel

Now the deck **moves**. Before writing the next concept, check the batch spread across all five
dimensions. Same format twice? Fine, IF the visual device is different. Same visual device twice?
Fine, IF the funnel job is different. Same on 3+ dimensions = same concept in a new dress.

## The sound-off test

Play the concept in your head with audio off. Now play the next concept with audio off. Do they
look meaningfully different? If both are "creator on couch with captions and product UI inserts,"
they're one concept. If one is a receipt printer spitting out receipts and the other is a wall
calendar transformation, they're two concepts.

A batch of 40 concepts should have 40 distinct visual identities that a director could sort into
piles by look.

## Specificity rule

Every concept needs at least **one number, timeframe, social detail, or tangible object** — prefer
two when credible. "7:30 AM, before the daycare drop-off," "day 3 of my cycle," "the sticker on
my Yeti," "a 30-day experiment." Numbers must be believable and compliant; never invent product
claims or fake stats.

## Ownability — "could a competitor run this?"

If a competitor could run the concept unchanged, it's too generic ("AI targeting helped me grow").
Make it ownable through:
- The specific situation ("my booth sold out but my profile sold nothing")
- The brand's real proof assets (its actual guarantee, actual free tool, actual track record)
- The brand's real product mechanic (dose adjustment, savings-card matching, unlimited support)

**Category-worn vehicle test (client feedback, the telehealth account, anti-aging).** Some vehicles pass the generic
test in the abstract but are worn out inside a specific category — every competitor has already
run them, so they read as stock even when well executed. Known worn-per-category examples:
- **Skincare / anti-aging:** the "serum graveyard" / drawer of failed products, the "my entire
  routine is one product" GRWM, the shelfie routine walk-through, the empties video.
- **Fitness / supplements:** the "what I eat in a day," the before/after mirror flex.
- **Growth / social tools:** the dashboard-number reveal as the whole ad.
Before assigning a vehicle, ask: has this brand's CATEGORY run this vehicle to death? If yes,
either pick a different vehicle or twist it hard enough that it no longer reads as the cliché
(the twist must live in the situation or trigger, not just in props). Vehicles worn in one
category can still be fresh in another — the check is per-category, not global. Extend this list
whenever a client flags a concept as "generic for the category."

## Language filter — 5th grade, not agency

Any of these phrases in the description, narrative, or design gets flagged for rewrite:

"positions..." · "reframes..." · "dramatises..." · "acts as..." · "quietly powers..." · "lands the
[x] benefit" · "proves the value" · "the identity lane" · "the emotional lane" · "the winner
category" · "the $XX CPA winner" · "top-of-funnel" (in slide copy — fine in strategy notes) ·
"performance lane" · "creative signal" · "hero concept" · "signature device" (fine in design;
never in description) · "the archetype" · "the mechanic" (fine in design; never in description).

The client is not a strategist. They shouldn't have to translate.

## Manufactured cleverness filter

Poetic lines, forced emotional beats, unnecessary props for aesthetic — cut. "The mug is empty at
close because time has passed" is manufactured. "The mug is empty at close" is fine as a shot
direction. The cleverness of a concept comes from recognizing something true about the customer
("vacation in seven days, out of BC"), not from writing pretty around a thin insight.

## UGC feasibility

Every concept must be shootable by:
- One creator at home
- With a smartphone
- With the product, the packaging, and normal household objects
- With one partner if a two-hander (a friend, a spouse, a sibling)

Elaborate sets, cast of actors, drone shots, hired locations, custom-built props — all flagged.
If the vehicle requires production scale (a mock TV commercial, a mock dating-app UI), the design
component must specify the low-fi UGC version (a phone screen playing a mock, not a full set).

## Compliance

Encode the brand's rules into every concept; never invent claims. Typical guardrails: exact offer
language (script phase anyway), health/medical support-framing only, realistic product screens,
never shame the audience, platform-word restrictions (e.g. don't say "followers"/"Instagram" where
prohibited — show the profile instead). Where personal-experience framing is required (BC surprise
benefits, HRT stories), the caveat must be visible on-screen, not just in VO.

**Qualify medical claims inside the concept itself (final-deck standard).** The client's finals
qualify every health claim at the concept level, not just at script/legal review:
- Benefit claims carry the qualifier in the beat: "certain birth-control methods *may* help with
  hormonal acne, *depending on the option and the person*" — never "birth control fixes acne."
- Comedy about outcomes is framed as PERCEPTION, not effect: "keep the joke about the husband's
  exaggerated perception, not a literal claim that HRT changes personality."
- Efficacy stats get visible clinical-study footnotes wherever the number appears on screen.
- Comparative claims name the mechanism, not a doctor's endorsement: "prescription-strength
  tretinoin, up to 20X stronger than OTC retinol" passes; "my derm would've prescribed the same
  thing" and "the one thing dermatologists actually agree on" fail — they put words in
  clinicians' mouths. Write the cleaner-sounding version only if the brand's docs literally
  support it.

## The review lens (for revising a deck)

Sort each concept:
- **Keep** — working.
- **Adjust** — right observation + creative leap, fix specificity / feasibility / narrative shape.
- **Replace** — no vehicle, insight-directly-to-ad, near-dupe of another concept, generic, or
  unshootable.

Re-check every survivor and replacement against the library and the five-dimension diversity
spread. Then run the full strategist gate on the revised set.

**Survival diff (after the client finalizes).** When the final client deck lands, compare it
against the generated batch: which concepts survived as-is, which were retitled, which were
replaced, and what the replacements have in common. Patterns found in the replacements become new
composition targets or craft rules (that's where the Batch Composition Targets section came from).
Ask the user for the final deck if they haven't shared it.

## Generate concepts to select, not to rescue

The benchmark is: **would a creative director put a check mark beside this and move directly into
refinement?** Not "could a strategist improve this." If the concept requires rescue, replace it.
The strategist gate exists to catch failures — not to become the concept generator.
