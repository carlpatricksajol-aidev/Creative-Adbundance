# Sourcing cheatsheet

Every action item and every cross-batch flag has a SOURCE line under the headline. The stronger and more specific the source, the more effective the flag reads in the room. This doc catalogs how to phrase citations for each type of material.

## The general shape

Cite the shortest string that lets a client immediately locate the source themselves:

```
SOURCE  ·  <material name>, <section or beat> [—  "verbatim quote if short"]
```

Keep it under two lines at 8pt italic. If the citation wraps to three lines you're citing too much — cite the section, don't paste the paragraph.

## By material type

### Brand pack / voice guide

Cite the section number if the document has one, and the specific rule verbatim.

- `Brand Pack v1.1 §06 Voice — never: "silent killer hiding in your body"`
- `Brand Pack v1.1 §08 Claims — prohibited: Treats gout, arthritis, carpal tunnel, pain or disease`
- `Brand Pack v1.1 §03 Typography (Geist Mono for labels)`
- `Brand Pack v1.1 §09 — Facts panel pending; no dose or serving figures ship until confirmed`

If the pack doesn't have numbered sections, use the heading verbatim:

- `Brand Voice guide — "Write like a clear editor, not a fear-based seller."`

### Script formula / creative reference

Cite by beat number if beats are numbered, otherwise by section name.

- `Script Formula, beat 5: "Enough to land the reframe. Not a biology lesson. Nothing cellular."`
- `Script Formula, beat 6: "Product enters late; stays a support layer. Never before beat 6."`
- `Script Formula — 12–15s cut runs beats 1, 3, 5, 8 only`

### Creative brief

Cite by field name and quote the target answer verbatim.

- `Company Creative Brief — Target: "50+, skinny fat, someone who looks like they buy our supplement"`
- `Company Creative Brief — Scoreboard primary target: "Schools (primarily high schools, but also middle schools, colleges), parks & municipal fields"`
- `Company Creative Brief — Deliverables: 6 video concepts + 3 hooks each`

### Kickoff / meeting notes / call transcripts

Speaker name + date is the strongest possible source — it lets the strategist read the flag out loud in the room and the client hears their own words back. Extract verbatim.

- `Kickoff notes 9/1: the client's founder — "We prefer to have some real characters in the ad. It just seems to talk to the consumer a little bit better."`
- `Kickoff notes 9/1: the client's marketing lead — "Someone that can sell it. you look too good for this."`
- `11/3 B1 Alignment: the client's CEO — "Shifting from a low upfront + subscription model to a higher upfront payment of about $15,000"`
- `Kickoff transcript 9/1: the client's head of digital — "Gout is the strongest converter… we do want to keep that angle and solidify it."`

If the speaker's role matters for authority, include it:

- `Kickoff notes 9/1: the client's head of digital — "click-through rate and CPMs are going to be the biggest determiners of success"`

### Client emails or Slack messages

Cite by sender and date. Verbatim if short, section if long.

- `the client founder's email 1/16 (deck comment) — "very new (beta) and I don't want too much of the creative focused on that"`
- `the client's head of digital, Slack 8/22 — auto vertical outperformance on B1 statics`

### Performance data

Cite by document name and the specific metric or callout.

- `Batch 1 Performance Insights — auto = #2 CPL vertical`
- `Batch 1 Performance Insights → "What we should test next: Video story arcs (problem, solution, proof)"`
- `Meta ads account (client-provided) — gout static winners running 40% below account CPL average`

### Website / product page

Cite the URL path or the specific claim.

- `the supplement client's site — product page, "Your Daily Defense Against Chronic Inflammation"`
- `the signage client's site, scoreboards page — "12 sports built-in"`
- `the lending client's site — "personal loans, debt consolidation, credit card refinancing"`

### Prior review deck / precedent

If a prior batch review addressed the same issue and the client has already agreed to it, cite that:

- `B1 Concept Alignment Review — Concept 4 audience flag (client accepted 8/22)`

## What NOT to write in a source line

- **"General consensus"** — no. If it's a real rule, it's written down somewhere.
- **"Industry best practice"** — no. This deck cites the client's own material, not generic marketing wisdom.
- **"CAB internal standard"** — occasionally OK for production principles (e.g. rights on stitched reactions), but only when there's a real internal doc backing it. Cite that doc.
- **A whole URL as the source** — the source line should be readable. Cite the doc name, not `https://docs.google.com/document/d/1abc…`.
- **"See attached"** — the client can't see attachments in a paid ad review. Cite the specific claim.

## When you can't find a source

If a concern feels real but you can't find a source, it's a flag for the strategist, not for the client. Either:

1. **Cut it from the deck.** If it's not sourceable, the strategist can't defend it in the room.
2. **Re-read the source materials.** You may have missed something — voice guides especially bury important rules.
3. **Escalate to the strategist as a "verify" open question.** For example, in the Executive Summary's ask-at-the-top card: "Does the brand pack have a stated position on X?" — the strategist asks the client, and if the answer is "no we haven't decided," you've just surfaced a real gap.

The rule is simple: no unsourced flags in the deck itself. Unsourced concerns become open questions for the strategist to raise, not flags for the client to defend.
